What we proved today

No root needed on Android 17

A throwaway probe app on your HP Googlebook 14 opened the AirPods' control channel, read their whole state and streamed head motion. The Bluetooth bug that forces LibrePods users to root is fixed in Android 17's Bluetooth module. One hidden Android call remains, reached with an Apache-2.0 library.

Check on the HP (Android 17, AirPods Pro 2 USB-C, A3048)Result
Public socket API createUsingSocketSettings(TYPE_L2CAP)Rejected: invalid socketType - 3. It only covers RFCOMM and LE.
Hidden createL2capSocket(0x1001) via HiddenApiBypassConnects in about 100 ms. No root, no Xposed.
Handshake and notification requestModel, firmware, capability list, 19 settings, battery (left 100 %, right 100 %), listening mode (Transparency), ear state, bud role.
Head motion (DEVMOTION6 sensor)Start acknowledged, then 181 samples in 7.1 s (25 Hz). Stops on request.
Features that need Apple's vendor IDHearing aid, transparency tuning, loud sound reduction and automatic switching need the host to claim Apple's Bluetooth vendor ID. That takes root, and new AirPods firmware drops such hosts every ~40 s anyway. PodLink leaves them out.

Details: docs/research/device-findings.md, librepods.md, ecosystem.md and android-platform.md in the repo.

What PodLink does

PodLink shows only what your AirPods report they support. The AirPods send a capability list when they connect, and the UI follows it, so AirPods 4 see a shorter page than AirPods Pro 3.

Design

The canvas has 10 screens so far: the Googlebook window (light, and a dark subpage), the head-gesture demo, the Quick Settings tile and its panel, notifications, first run, and four phone screens. Listening modes are clickable, and in Play you can click the demo shape to step through its states.

One quiet window

On a Googlebook, the AirPods sit on the left (battery, listening mode, two main switches) and settings on the right. The header row sits under the system caption, painted the same colour. Nothing floats.

Phone: one column

The same pieces stacked. Sections open as pages. Taller rows, bigger targets.

Tile and panel

The tile shows the current mode and battery. A tap opens a small panel (the "subpage"), and a long-press opens PodLink.

Yes and No

The demo's shape morphs into a scalloped Yes or a four-lobed No (Material 3 Expressive shapes). Live motion traces, counters and a sensitivity switch sit beside it.

Colour and art

Wallpaper colours where the device offers them, otherwise PodLink teal. The earbuds and case are our own simple drawing: no Apple images or fonts.

Light touch

No overlay permission and no accessibility service. Head motion only on demand. No internet permission.

Architecture

Stack

Kotlin, Jetpack Compose, Material 3 Expressive (material3 1.5 alpha, pinned like Summa), Material 3 Adaptive for the two-pane layout, DataStore. Package io.github.kuscher.podlink, target SDK 37, one APK for x86_64 and Arm (no native code).

Permissions

Nearby devices (connect, and scan without location), notifications, foreground service for connected devices, companion device presence. On phones, optionally answer calls. No internet, no overlays, no accessibility service.

Connection flow

AirPods connect → the companion-device presence event starts the service → open the channel → handshake → subscribe → state reaches the UI, tile and notification. On disconnect the service stops and keeps the last-known battery.

Clean room

LibrePods is GPL-3.0. PodLink's code is our own, written from protocol facts (byte layouts and opcodes) and our own packet captures, with LibrePods credited for the research. No code, docs text, fonts or images copied.

Build, test, back up

  • A ./podlink helper builds, installs and drives the app on the HP over the Terminal's adb socket, like ./summa. Nothing launches or steals focus while you're working.
  • Protocol and gesture tests run on the VM. Device checks run on the HP (Arm) and the ASUS (x86_64). No emulators on the VM.
  • GitHub Actions runs the tests and a debug build on every push. Releases are signed builds from a v* tag with the APK and SHA-256 attached.
  • Commit and push to kuscher/podlink (private) at every milestone. The release key lives in ~/.config/podlink with a copy in private storage, never in git.
  • Publishing: GitHub releases first, then googlebook.studio, then a Google Play kit (listing, screenshots, data safety) like Summa's.

Risks

RiskWhat we do about it
The hidden socket call breaks in a future Android, or Play objects to itKeep it in one small transport class. Watch AOSP for public classic-L2CAP support. Fall back to a battery-only mode over BLE.
Older phones without Android 17's Bluetooth fixDetect the failed channel, explain it, and keep battery and nearby alerts working over BLE.
AirPods firmware changesParse defensively, drive the UI from the capability list, and keep recorded packet traces as regression tests. Mid-September firmware makes the AirPods' hands-free link flap on all Android devices. That isn't ours to fix, but PodLink should say so rather than look broken.
AirPods owned by your iPhone or MacHead motion flows only to the device the AirPods currently serve. Show "In use on another device" and never grab them without asking.
The name"PodsLink – AirPods Battery" is on Play with over a million installs, one letter away and already advertising noise control and head gestures. pod.link is a podcast service, and a GitHub project "Podlink" (AirPods) started this month. HearLink is Philips' hearing-aid brand and app (Philips HearLink 2, 100k+ installs), and "AI EarLink" is an earbud companion on Play, so both are taken too. Renaming before the first release is cheap; "EarDeck" came up clear in a quick search.
Trademarks"AirPods" only as a description ("for AirPods"), never in the app name, plus a clear non-affiliation note. No Apple images, fonts or animations, and avoid "Find My" and "Live Listen".
Another AirPods app holds the channelOnly one app at a time can open the control channel (CAPod and LibrePods block each other). Detect it and say which app to close, instead of failing silently.
Battery drainThe service runs only while the AirPods are connected. Head motion streams only on demand. The BLE scan uses low power with a hardware filter.
